Keeping Your Grand Prairie Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.
