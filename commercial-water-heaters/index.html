When to Call for Commercial Water Heaters

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank
  • Hot water running out during your busiest hour
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time
  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency
  • Scalding bursts or wild temperature swings at showers or sinks
  • Water temperatures that worry you before a health inspection
  • Rumbling, popping, or banging from a heavily used tank
  • A recirculation loop that no longer seems to do its job

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Inspect anode rods regularly, since heavy use consumes them faster
  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
  • Schedule upgrades during slow periods instead of waiting for a breakdown
  • Leave space around commercial heaters for airflow and safe servicing
  • Flush commercial tanks more often than residential ones, often every few months in heavy use or hard water
  • Record fixture temperatures regularly, especially before inspections
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
