Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Fort Worth

Many early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.
