A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. Most failures trace back to one of those parts. In Burleson, TX, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If you ever smell gas, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Burleson technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

Some gas heaters need a quick part swap, and some need an honest conversation. We handle both. Thermocouples, flame sensors, pilot assemblies, hot surface igniters, gas valves, and burner cleaning are routine repairs, and we correct venting faults and flush sediment during the same visit when needed. If the tank is leaking, rusting through, or past about ten to twelve years with a major failure, we will explain why replacement makes more sense. Our installations cover like-for-like swaps and upgrades to power vent or high-efficiency models, with correct gas line sizing, sediment traps, expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic strapping wherever required. When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Start with the hazards. Every gas water heater involves fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and working through them by guesswork is slow and costly.

Professional service gets you a correct answer and a safe appliance. We confirm thermocouple output, gas pressure, burner flame, and draft with real instruments, not trial and error. Every gas connection we make is tested for leaks before we leave, and vent connectors are secured with the right slope and clearances. When a replacement is needed, we size the new heater by first-hour rating, handle the permit where one is required, and add the expansion tank, sediment trap, drain pan, or seismic straps your home needs. You end up with hot water and peace of mind.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
